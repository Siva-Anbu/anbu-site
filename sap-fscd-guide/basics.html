FSCD basics, with a Danish example

Simple English, one Danish insurance example from start to finish, and pictures of the key screens.

How to read this guide

Every idea is explained twice: first in plain words, then with the same insurance customer, Mette. Read section 3 (the posting document) slowly. Once that clicks, the rest of FSCD makes sense.

Meet the example

All names, numbers and codes below are made up for learning. Your company's codes and screens will look slightly different, but the ideas are the same.

About the tax line

Danish non-life premiums carry a small insurance tax (skadesforsikringsafgift), and motor liability cover also carries a separate, larger tax. To keep the example simple we use one 1.1% tax line. Your project will have its own tax rules.

1. What SAP FSCD is, in simple words

SAP FSCD stands for Financial Services Collections and Disbursements. In simple words: it is the part of SAP that keeps track of money coming in to the insurer and money going out of it.

Money in (collections)

Premiums customers pay, late fees, bounced-payment charges.

Mette pays DKK 8,088 for her car insurance.

Money out (disbursements)

Claim payments, premium refunds, agent commissions.

ABC pays Mette DKK 25,000 after her car accident, and pays Lars DKK 800 commission.

A simple picture: FSCD is like a bank statement for every customer. Each line on the statement is something the customer owes or is owed, and every payment is ticked off against those lines.

The policy system decides how much the premium is. FSCD does not calculate premiums. It takes the amount, bills it, collects it, chases it if unpaid, and records everything for the accountants.

Two technical facts worth knowing:

  • FSCD is built on FI-CA (Contract Accounts Receivable and Payable), the SAP engine for companies with millions of small, repeating payments, like telecoms and electricity companies. FSCD adds insurance features on top.
  • FSCD is a sub-ledger: it keeps the detail for each customer and sends daily totals to the company's main accounts (the general ledger, FI-GL). Finance sees "DKK 20 million premium billed today"; FSCD knows that DKK 8,088 of it is Mette's.

Why it matters for a BA: almost every FSCD requirement answers one of four questions.

QuestionMette example
1. What does the customer owe or get, and how is it recorded?Premium of DKK 8,088 is billed on 01.04.2026.
2. How is it collected or paid?Direct debit through Betalingsservice from her bank account on 15.04.2026.
3. What happens when it goes wrong?The debit bounces; a DKK 100 fee is added and a reminder goes out.
4. How does it reach accounts and reports?The premium appears in the general ledger and in the finance team's daily report.

Business Partner (BP)

In simple words: any person or company ABC deals with. One record, stored once, used everywhere.

Example: Mette is a BP. So is Lars the agent, the garage (autoværksted) that repairs her car, and the clinic in a health claim. One person can play several roles: if Mette later becomes an agent, she stays the same BP.

Contract Account

In simple words: the customer's "money account" at ABC, like a wallet. All bills and payments for the customer land here. It also holds the rules: how she pays (Betalingsservice), her bank details, and how reminders work.

Example: Mette's contract account 200008731 holds her motor policy. If she buys home insurance (indboforsikring) later, it can sit in the same account, so she gets one combined bill.

Contract / Insurance Object (the policy)

In simple words: the actual insurance policy, as FSCD sees it. Every amount is tagged with the policy it belongs to.

Example: MOT-2026-004512. If Mette asks "what did I pay for my car policy?", you filter by this policy number.

Posting Document

In simple words: the system's permanent record of one money event, like a printed bill or receipt in a file. Every time money is owed, paid, refunded or charged, FSCD creates a new posting document with a number. Section 3 shows one in detail.

Example: document 100000452311 records "Mette owes DKK 8,088 premium for her motor policy, due 15.04.2026".

Open Item

In simple words: a line from a posting document that is not settled yet. "Open" = still waiting for money (or still waiting to be paid out).

Example: from 01.04 until Mette pays, her DKK 8,088 is an open item. After payment it becomes a cleared item.

Clearing

In simple words: ticking off a bill because a payment matched it. The payment and the bill close each other.

Example: Mette's DKK 8,088 payment arrives and is matched to the premium bill. Both are now cleared and her balance is zero.

Main and Sub Transaction

In simple words: two short codes on every line that say what kind of money it is. The main transaction is the category; the sub transaction is the detail. Accounting uses these codes to decide which ledger account to post to.

Example (made-up codes): main 6100 Premium with sub 0010 Net premium for DKK 8,000, and sub 0020 Insurance tax for DKK 88. A claim payout might be main 6200 Claim.

Payment Method

In simple words: how the money moves.

Example: Mette pays by direct debit through Betalingsservice. Others pay by MobilePay, bank transfer, Dankort/card or a payment slip (indbetalingskort). Claims are usually paid by bank transfer to the customer or straight to the garage.

Payment Run

In simple words: a scheduled batch job (often nightly) that picks up everything due and creates the bank files: debits to collect premiums, credits to pay out claims.

Example: the 15.04 night run finds Mette's premium is due and adds her DKK 8,088 debit to the Betalingsservice file, along with thousands of others.

Return (bounced payment)

In simple words: the bank sends a payment back. FSCD undoes the clearing, so the bill is open again, and may add a charge.

Example: Mette's account has too little money on 15.04. The bank returns the debit on 18.04. Her DKK 8,088 is open again, plus a DKK 100 fee.

Dunning

In simple words: the step-by-step reminder process for overdue money. Each step (level) is stronger than the last.

Example: Level 1 email and SMS on day 14 overdue, level 2 reminder letter (rykker) in e-Boks on day 30, level 3 "your cover will stop" notice on day 45.

Collection (agency)

In simple words: when reminders fail, the debt goes to a recovery team or outside debt-collection agency (inkasso), or the policy is cancelled for non-payment.

Example: if Mette never paid, after level 3 the policy would be cancelled and the unpaid amount handed to recovery. In Denmark the Insurance Contracts Act (forsikringsaftaleloven) requires a formal written reminder with a deadline before cover can stop, so dunning rules must follow it.

Disbursement

In simple words: any money ABC pays out.

Example: Mette's DKK 25,000 claim, a refund if she cancels mid-year, and Lars's DKK 800 commission.

Reconciliation Key

In simple words: a "batch label" stuck on a group of postings so finance can check that FSCD totals match the general ledger.

Example: all premiums billed on 01.04.2026 carry key 260401-PREM-01. Finance checks that key's total in FSCD equals the total in the ledger.

Posting Date vs Document Date

In simple words: document date is when the business event happened; posting date is the date it counts in the accounts. They are often the same, but not always.

Example: Mette pays a payment slip at her bank on 31.12.2026 (document date), but the money is only booked at ABC on 04.01.2027 (posting date), so it counts in the new financial year (January), not December.

Event (FKK event)

In simple words: a hook where developers can add company-specific logic without changing SAP's own code.

Example: "Before paying any claim above DKK 250,000, check that a second approver signed it."

3. The posting document, explained with screens

One line: a posting document is FSCD's permanent record of one money event, like a numbered bill or receipt in a file that can never be torn up, only cancelled with another document.

Why it matters: when anyone asks "why does Mette owe this?" or "where did this DKK 100 come from?", the answer is always a posting document. Learn to read one and you can investigate almost any issue.

What each number means:

  • 1

    Document number: the unique ID of this money event. Quote it in emails and tickets: "please check document 100000452311".

  • 2

    Document type: what kind of event this is, here a premium bill. Others: payment, claim payout, return, reversal.

  • 3

    Document date: when the business event happened (the policy started billing on 01.04).

  • 4

    Posting date: which day and accounting month this counts in. Finance cares about this one.

  • 5

    Currency: Danish kroner.

  • 6

    Reconciliation key: the batch label finance uses to match FSCD totals with the ledger.

  • 7

    Created by: who or what made it. Here the policy-system interface, not a person. Very useful when investigating.

  • 8

    Reversed: whether this document was later cancelled by another document.

  • 9

    BP: who owes the money: Mette.

  • 10

    Contract account: which "wallet" the bill sits in.

  • 11

    Contract: which policy it belongs to.

  • 12

    Main / Sub transaction: what kind of money: net premium (0010) and insurance tax (0020). They decide which ledger accounts are used.

  • 13

    Amount: how much. One document can have many lines; here two lines total DKK 8,088.

  • 14

    Due date: when the money must be paid. The payment run and dunning both look at this date.

  • 15

    Status: Open means not paid yet. These two lines are Mette's open items.

Posting document vs open item: the key difference

The posting document is the whole bill (header + all lines). An open item is one unpaid line inside it. When Mette pays, the document stays exactly as it is forever; only the status of its lines changes from Open to Cleared.

Mistakes are never deleted

If a premium is posted twice by mistake, you cannot delete the second document. You reverse it: FSCD creates a new document with the opposite amounts, and both stay visible. This "no rubbing out" rule is what makes FSCD trustworthy for finance and auditors.

4. Where FSCD sits among the other systems

FSCD never works alone. Other systems tell it what happened; FSCD handles the money.

SystemIts job, in simple wordsMette example
Policy administration (SAP FS-PM or a non-SAP system)Sells and changes policies, calculates premiumsCalculates DKK 8,000 premium and sends it to FSCD to bill
Claims (SAP FS-CM or non-SAP)Checks and approves claimsApproves her DKK 25,000 claim and tells FSCD to pay it
Commission (FS-ICM)Works out agent and broker commissionCalculates Lars's DKK 800 and sends it to FSCD to pay
FI-CAThe engine underneath FSCDProvides documents, payment runs, dunning, clearing
General ledger (FI-GL)The company's main accountsReceives the daily total of all premiums, including hers
Business Partner (SAP BP)Central customer and partner dataHolds Mette's name, address, CPR number and bank details
Banks and payment channelsActually move the moneyBetalingsservice debit file goes out via Nets; bank return file comes back
Reporting / BIReports for finance and managementHer bounce shows up in the "failed collections" report
  • Many requirements are really interface requirements. Policy and claims are often non-SAP systems. So the BA question is often: what data comes in, in what format, at what time, and what happens if it fails?
  • FSCD is the "money truth". If the policy system says Mette's premium is unpaid but FSCD says it is paid, finance trusts FSCD.

5. Everyday processes, told as Mette's story

5.1 Money in: collecting Mette's premium

  1. 01.04.2026: Billing. The policy system sends "bill DKK 8,000 + insurance tax". FSCD creates the premium posting document. Doc 100000452311, open items DKK 8,088.
  2. 01.04.2026, night: To accounts. The day's premiums, including Mette's, are summed and posted to the general ledger.
  3. 15.04.2026: Payment run. The due date arrives; the nightly run adds her DKK 8,088 to the Betalingsservice debit file for the bank. The items are cleared, assuming the money will arrive. Doc 100000452640.
  4. 18.04.2026: Return. The bank sends the debit back: insufficient funds. FSCD reverses the clearing, so the items are open again, and adds a DKK 100 fee. Doc 100000452877. Owes DKK 8,188.
  5. 02.05.2026: Dunning level 1. 14 days overdue: an email and SMS reminder goes out automatically.
  6. 06.05.2026: Payment. Mette pays DKK 8,188 by MobilePay. The payment file is loaded, FSCD matches the payment to her open items using the policy number in the reference, and clears them. Doc 100000453190. Balance DKK 0.
  7. If she had never paid: level 2 reminder letter in e-Boks on day 30, level 3 "your cover will stop" notice on day 45, then the policy is cancelled for non-payment and the balance goes to recovery, following company rules and the Insurance Contracts Act.
What if the payment can't be matched?

If Mette forgets the policy number in her MobilePay message, FSCD can't tell whose money it is. It parks the DKK 8,188 in clarification (post-processing), and an operations user finds the right account and assigns it by hand. Reducing these is a classic BA improvement topic.

5.2 Money out: paying Mette's claim

  1. 10.07.2026: Accident. Mette's car is damaged; she files claim CLM-2026-118.
  2. 20.07.2026: Payable created. The claims system approves DKK 25,000 and sends it to FSCD. FSCD creates a posting document with a credit (money ABC owes her). Doc 100000461005.
  3. 20.07.2026: Checks. FSCD checks her bank details are valid, there is no payment block (for example a fraud review), and whether she owes anything. If she still owed the DKK 8,188 premium, it could be offset: she would receive DKK 16,812 instead.
  4. 22.07.2026: Payment run. The outgoing run puts DKK 25,000 in the bank-transfer file to her account. Doc 100000461322. Paid.
  5. If the bank rejects it (for example a wrong registration number or account number), the payable re-opens, and someone corrects the bank details and pays again.

5.3 Money out: paying Lars's commission

The commission system calculates 10% of Mette's DKK 8,000 net premium = DKK 800 and sends it to FSCD. FSCD posts it as a payable on Lars's contract account (he is a Business Partner too). The monthly commission run on 10.05.2026 pays it by bank transfer. A common business rule: only pay commission once the customer's premium is actually received. In our story that's 06.05, so Lars is paid in the 10.05 run, not before.

6.2 What a BA typically does in FSCD

  • Writes requirements and user stories. Example: "As a collections officer, I want return fees waived automatically when the bank return reason is 'bank error', so customers aren't charged unfairly."
  • Does fit-gap analysis. Can standard FSCD already do it (configuration), or does it need custom development?
  • Investigates incidents. "Why was Mette charged DKK 100?" FPL9 → find document 100000452877 → it's a return with reason "insufficient funds" → the fee is correct.
  • Documents rules. Dunning days and levels, write-off limits, which payment methods each product allows.
  • Supports testing. Writes scenarios, prepares test policies, joins UAT, logs defects.
  • Translates. Business teams (finance, claims, operations) on one side; SAP consultants and developers on the other.

6.3 Questions to ask for any new requirement

  1. Which customers and contract accounts does it affect? (All motor customers? Only Betalingsservice ones?)
  2. Which main and sub transactions are involved? (Premium? Insurance tax? A new fee?)
  3. Is money coming in or going out? Which payment method?
  4. What are the amounts, currency and tax rules? (Is insurance tax charged on the new fee?)
  5. What are the dates? (Due date, grace period, bank cut-off times)
  6. What happens when it fails? (Bounce, rejection, partial payment, duplicate)
  7. Does it change ledger postings or reports? Who in finance must agree?
  8. Which system sends or receives the data, and in what format?
  9. How will we test it, and which edge cases matter most?

7. A 30-day learning plan

About 45 to 60 minutes a day is enough. Pair every reading day with a look at real data in your project's test system.

WeekFocusPractical task
1Sections 1 to 3: building blocks and the posting documentPick one test policy. In FPL9 find its BP, contract account and items. Open one document in FPE3 and name every field, like screen 1 above.
2Money in: billing, payment, clearing, returns, dunningFollow one premium from bill to cleared. Then find one bounced payment and explain its documents, like screen 2.
3Money out: claims, refunds, commission, blocks, GL transferTrace one claim payout from payable to bank file. Find its ledger posting.
4Your project: interfaces, custom logic, open requirements, incidentsRead three recent tickets and label each with one of the four questions from section 1.

Ask your team in the first two weeks

  • Which systems feed FSCD (policy, claims, commission), and through which interfaces?
  • Which payment methods and dunning procedures do we use, per product?
  • What are our real main/sub transaction codes for premium, insurance tax, fees and claims?
  • Is there a process map, configuration document or glossary I can read?
  • Which custom developments or events exist, and who owns them?
  • Who in finance is the go-to person for reconciliation questions?
  • Which reports do users rely on most?
