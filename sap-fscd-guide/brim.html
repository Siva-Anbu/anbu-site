SAP BRIM: billing for new kinds of insurance

What SAP BRIM is, how its parts fit together, and how it works next to FSCD when an insurer sells pay-per-use, subscription or partner products. Told with Mette's new pay-per-km car insurance.

Before you start

This lesson builds on Lesson 1 (contract accounts, open items) and Lesson 4 (postings and the general ledger). All names, prices and codes are made up for learning. SAP has renamed parts of BRIM over the years, so confirm the product names and versions on your own project.

Mette tries something new

ABC Forsikring launches ABC Km: car insurance where you pay per kilometre you drive. Mette works from home now and drives much less, so she switches. Her price is:

Base feeDKK 199 a month, whatever she drives
Per kilometreDKK 0.45 for every km, counted by a small box in her car
Her May820 km driven → 199 + 369 = DKK 568
BillOne monthly bill, paid by Betalingsservice as before

This product is hard for a classic premium setup: the amount is different every month and it depends on thousands of small usage events from the car. This is exactly what SAP BRIM was built for. All amounts in this lesson include tax, to keep the numbers simple.

1. What is SAP BRIM?

BRIM stands for Billing and Revenue Innovation Management. It's SAP's set of tools for companies that bill for subscriptions and usage: telecom companies, energy, streaming, car sharing, and more and more insurers. In newer SAP material you'll also see names like SAP S/4HANA subscription billing or quote-to-cash for subscriptions, but most project teams still say "BRIM".

Classic insurance billing (FSCD)BRIM-style billing
Premium is calculated by the policy systemPrice is calculated from usage, as it happens
Fixed schedule: yearly, quarterly, monthlyAmount changes every period
A few postings per customer per yearThousands of small events per customer per month
One insurer, one customerOften partners too: car makers, phone companies, shops

2. The parts of BRIM, in simple words

BRIM is not one screen. It's a chain of components, each with one job. Think of a factory line: raw events go in on the left, and a bill and an accounting entry come out on the right.

SOM sets up the contract; CM, CC and CI turn usage into a bill; FI-CA collects the money. RAR (revenue accounting) can sit at the end for some products.

3. BRIM and FSCD: same accounting heart

Here's the most important idea for an FSCD BA. FSCD and BRIM both sit on FI-CA (Contract Accounting). FSCD is the insurance version of FI-CA; BRIM's invoicing (CI) posts into FI-CA. So in an insurer, both can post into the same business partner and the same contract account.

Different roads in, the same road out. Everything you learned in Lessons 1 to 4 still applies once the bill reaches FI-CA.

What this means for you

Mette can get one combined bill or one Betalingsservice debit for her home insurance and her ABC Km. A payment clears items from both. Dunning, returns, write-offs and the GL transfer work exactly as before. The new things to learn are mostly before FI-CA: contracts, rating and invoicing.

4. The journey of one month of kilometres

Let's follow Mette's May from the car to her bank account.

  1. 01.05 · contract: SOM creates the ABC Km subscription. In FI-CA/CI this becomes a provider contract linked to Mette's business partner and contract account, and in CC a matching contract with her price plan.
  2. During May · usage: each trip sends a record (date, car, km). Mediation (CM) checks it: right car? already received? Then it passes it to Charging.
  3. During May · rating: Charging (CC) prices each trip: 14 km × DKK 0.45 = DKK 6.30. The priced result is sent to CI as a billable item.
  4. 31.05 · billing: the billing run in CI collects all of Mette's billable items for May (63 trips, 820 km, DKK 369) plus the base fee (DKK 199) and creates a billing document.
  5. 01.06 · invoicing: the invoicing run turns the billing document into an invoice and posts it in FI-CA. Now there is an open item of DKK 568, due 15.06.
  6. 15.06 · payment: FSCD/FI-CA's payment run collects it by Betalingsservice (Lesson 2). If it bounces, the return and dunning steps from Lesson 3 kick in.

The customer never sees 63 lines unless you want them to: the invoice can show a summary and a trip list as an attachment.

Billable item

In simple words: one priced line waiting to be billed. It knows the customer, the contract, the amount and what it's for.

Example: "Mette, ABC Km, trip on 12.05, 14 km, DKK 6.30". If something is wrong (no contract found, say), it lands in an error status and must be fixed before billing.

Billing vs invoicing

In simple words: billing collects and adds up the items; invoicing makes the bill the customer sees and posts it to accounting. They are two separate runs in CI.

Example: billing on 31.05 makes the billing document; invoicing on 01.06 makes the DKK 568 invoice and the open item. Invoicing can also put the home insurance premium on the same bill.

Provider contract

In simple words: the FI-CA/CI record of "what this customer subscribed to". It ties the business partner, contract account and pricing together, so billable items know where to go.

Example: Mette's provider contract for ABC Km, valid from 01.05.2026. If she cancels on 31.08, usage after that date is rejected.

5. Partners: when someone else sells the insurance

Insurers increasingly sell through partners: a phone shop offers screen insurance, an electricity company offers home insurance, a car maker offers insurance with a new car. BRIM can bill the customer and settle money with the partner.

In practice the partner often keeps its share and pays the net amount. Either way, BRIM tracks what each side owes.

For a BA, partner deals raise new questions: who owns the customer? Who sends the bill? Who chases unpaid premium? How often is the partner settled, and on which report? BRIM's partner settlement handles the money; the business rules must come from you.

6. Pricing and products: who decides the price?

In classic FSCD, the policy system calculates the premium and FSCD only bills it. In BRIM, the price for usage is calculated in Convergent Charging, based on the price plan in the contract. That's a big change in who owns what:

QuestionClassic FSCD productBRIM product
Where is the price calculated?Policy system (underwriting rules)Charging (CC), from the price plan; underwriting still sets the risk rate
Where is the "contract"?Policy in the policy system; insurance object in FSCDSubscription in SOM; provider contract in CI; contract in CC
What is billed?Premium instalments on a scheduleBase fees plus usage, gathered each period
How do changes work?Endorsement in the policy system, new premium sent to FSCDChange the subscription in SOM; new price applies from a date
Many insurers use both

A common design: the policy system stays the master for the insurance contract and the risk price, and BRIM handles the usage, subscription and partner billing around it. A BA's job is to draw that line clearly: which system owns which data, and what happens when they disagree.

7. Revenue accounting: a word of caution

BRIM is often sold together with RAR (Revenue Accounting and Reporting), which helps companies follow the accounting standard IFRS 15 for when to show revenue. But insurance contracts follow IFRS 17, not IFRS 15. So for an insurer:

  • The insurance premium itself usually goes to the insurer's IFRS 17 and actuarial systems, as in Lesson 4, not to RAR.
  • Non-insurance services sold alongside, for example a paid driving-app subscription or roadside assistance sold as a service, may fall under IFRS 15 and could use RAR.
  • Always ask finance which standard applies to each part of a new product before the design is fixed.

9. BA toolkit for BRIM

9.1 Questions to ask for a usage-based product

  1. What is the usage event (a trip, a day abroad, a device), where does it come from, and how often?
  2. How is a usage event linked to the right customer and contract? What happens to events with no contract?
  3. What is the price plan: base fee, price per unit, tiers, caps (for example "never more than DKK 900 a month")?
  4. What is the billing period and the cut-off for late events (a trip from 31.05 that arrives on 03.06)?
  5. Does the product appear on a combined bill with classic policies?
  6. Is there a partner? Who bills, who collects, who is paid what, and how often?
  7. Which parts are insurance (IFRS 17) and which are services (maybe IFRS 15)?
