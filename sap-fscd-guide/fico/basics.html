FICO basics for an insurer, with a Danish example

What SAP FI and CO are, how an insurance company is set up in SAP, and how one journal entry works. Told with a Danish non-life insurer, Nordica Forsikring, and one rent payment.

Before you start

This is the first lesson of the FICO course, so you need no SAP or accounting background. If you already took the FSCD course, you know how premiums and claims are handled in the sub-ledger. This course is about the general ledger behind it. All names, amounts, account numbers and codes below are made up for learning.

Meet the example: Nordica Forsikring A/S

Nordica Forsikring A/S is a Danish non-life insurer (skadesforsikring) in Copenhagen. It sells motor, home and contents, travel and small business insurance. In SAP it is company code NF01 and it keeps its books in Danish kroner (DKK). The supervisor is Finanstilsynet (the Danish FSA), and the tax agency is Skattestyrelsen.

You are Sofie Holm, a new business analyst (BA) in the finance team. In this course you follow Nordica through 2026, one business event at a time.

1. What SAP FICO is, in simple words

SAP is one big system that runs a whole company. It is split into modules, one per business area. FICO is two modules that work as a pair:

FI: Financial Accounting

In simple words: the company's official books. Every krone in and out is recorded so the company can produce its balance sheet and its profit and loss statement (P&L), and report to the authorities. It looks outward: Finanstilsynet, Skattestyrelsen, auditors and owners read it.

Example: after year end, Peter Lund sends Nordica's annual report and statutory reports to Finanstilsynet. The numbers come from FI.

CO: Controlling

In simple words: the company's internal view of the same money. It answers "which department spent what?" and "which line of business makes money?". It looks inward: managers read it to make decisions.

Example: Jonas Krogh wants to know if motor or home insurance earns more. FI says "the company made a result". CO splits it by line of business.

1.1 Where FICO sits in an insurer

Most postings in FI and CO are not typed by finance people. They are created automatically when another system does its job: the policy system bills a policy, the claims system pays a claim, payroll pays salaries. Every one of these events has a money effect, and that effect lands in FI and CO.

The most important feed is SAP FS-CD. It collects premiums and pays claims and commission, one policyholder at a time, and sends daily totals to the general ledger. You can read how FS-CD works in the FSCD course.

Green: systems that send postings. Orange: reports built from those postings. Ordinary suppliers (rent, IT, consultants) are posted directly in FI accounts payable.

This is why a FICO BA must understand the other systems a little. When Jonas asks "why are claims costs high this month?", the answer may start with a claims payment in FS-CD, not with anything finance did. Claims payments to garages and policyholders go through the claims system and FS-CD, not through FI accounts payable.

2. How an insurer is set up in SAP

Before anyone can post a single krone, SAP needs to know the organisational structure: which legal company it is, which accounts it uses, how its year is divided and how managers want to see costs. These settings are made once, at the start of a project, and are hard to change later. That is why BAs are asked about them early.

Why special periods?

After December is finished, the auditor or the actuary may still find corrections, for example a changed reserve estimate. Instead of reopening December, they are posted in periods 13 to 16 of the same year. December stays clean, and the annual figures still include the corrections.

3. Debit, credit and the GL account

3.1 Debit and credit in one minute

Accounting uses double entry: every business event is written down at least twice, once on the debit side of one account and once on the credit side of another, for the same total. Debit and credit do not mean "good" and "bad". They are simply the left and right side of an account.

Kind of accountGoes up with aGoes down with aNordica example
Assets (what the company owns)DebitCreditMoney in Danske Bank, premiums customers still owe, the Valby office fit-out
Liabilities (what it owes)CreditDebitUnpaid supplier invoices, insurance tax owed to Skattestyrelsen, reserves for future claims
Equity (owners' share)CreditDebitShare capital, profit kept from earlier years
Expenses (costs)DebitCreditRent, telephony, depreciation, claims paid
Revenue (income)CreditDebitGross written premium, investment income

A "T-account" is how accountants sketch an account on paper: debits on the left, credits on the right.

How SAP shows it

In SAP reports a debit is a positive number and a credit is a negative number. So in the bank account's line items you will see -50,000.00 for the rent. Every document must add up to 0.00, otherwise SAP refuses to post it.

3.2 The GL account (FS00)

A GL account (general ledger account) is one "box" in the books where amounts of one kind are collected: bank, rent, premiums. You create and view it in transaction FS00. Every GL account is one of two big kinds:

Balance sheet account

In simple words: things the company owns or owes on a given day. The balance carries forward into the next year.

Example: the bank balance on 31.12.2026 is the opening balance on 01.01.2027.

P&L account

In simple words: income and costs for a period. At year end the total result moves to a retained earnings account in equity, and the P&L accounts start again from zero.

Example: rent for 2026 adds up to DKK 600,000 (12 × 50,000). On 01.01.2027 account 470000 starts at zero.

Reconciliation account

In simple words: a summary account in the GL for a whole group of policyholders or suppliers. You never post to it directly. When Maria posts an invoice to a supplier, SAP updates the supplier's own account and account 160000 at the same moment. In the same way FS-CD updates 140000 when it bills premiums. So the GL always agrees with the detailed lists.

Example: if Nordica owes 40 suppliers a total of DKK 900,000, account 160000 shows 900,000 and the 40 supplier accounts add up to the same 900,000.

In S/4HANA, FS00 also holds the cost element setting that CO needs. A P&L account such as Rent is a primary cost element (the cost comes from outside the company). An account that only moves cost inside the company, such as 940000, is a secondary cost element and only lives in CO.

3.3 Insurance-specific accounts, simply

Premium receivables (140000)

In simple words: what policyholders owe Nordica for premiums already billed. The details (who owes what, due dates) live in FS-CD. FS-CD sends daily totals to this account.

Example: on 01.03 FS-CD bills Hygge Hjem DKK 121,320 for policy ERH-2026-0042. The total lands on 140000.

Unearned premium reserve, UPR (250000)

In simple words: Nordica is paid for a full year, but each month only covers one twelfth of the cover. Written premium is what was billed. Earned premium is the part for the time that has already passed. The rest is a debt of service to the customer: the UPR (præmiehensættelser).

Example: Hygge Hjem's premium is DKK 120,000 for 12 months. After March, DKK 10,000 is earned and DKK 110,000 is unearned. Lesson 3 shows the postings.

Claims reserves (260000)

In simple words: money set aside for claims that have happened but are not fully paid. RBNS means "reported but not settled": a claim is known and still open. IBNR means "incurred but not reported": it has happened, but nobody has told us yet. The actuary, Line Madsen, estimates both.

Example: a storm damages a shop on 28.03 and the claim is reported on 02.04. At 31.03 it is IBNR; after 02.04 it is RBNS.

Insurance tax payable (290100)

In simple words: premiums carry a tax. In this course we use a simplified 1.1% skadesforsikringsafgift. Nordica collects it from the customer and pays it to Skattestyrelsen. Until it is paid, it is a debt. (Motor liability insurance has its own separate tax; we only mention it.)

Example: 1.1% of DKK 120,000 is DKK 1,320.

No VAT on premiums, and no input VAT account for purchases

Insurance is VAT-exempt in Denmark (momsfritaget). Nordica charges no VAT on premiums. In general it also cannot deduct the VAT it pays on purchases, so that VAT becomes part of the cost. Financial companies pay a payroll tax (lønsumsafgift) instead. This is why Nordica's chart of accounts has no "input VAT" account for purchases. Always confirm the exact rules with your tax and finance team.

4. The journal entry, explained with a screen

A journal entry (in SAP: an accounting document) is the permanent record of one business event. It has a header (information about the whole document) and two or more line items (one per account touched). Once posted it is never deleted. A mistake is fixed by posting a reversal document.

The story: on 25.02.2026 the landlord sends the rent invoice for March. On 01.03.2026 Maria Berg pays DKK 50,000 from Danske Bank and posts it with transaction FB50 (enter a GL account document). To keep it simple, the rent is shown without VAT. Sofie opens the result in FB03 (Display Document).

What each number means:

  • 1

    Document number: the unique ID of this entry inside company code NF01 and fiscal year 2026. Numbers come from a number range per document type. Always quote it: "please check document 100000017 in NF01, 2026".

  • 2

    Company code: whose books it is in. NF01, Nordica Forsikring.

  • 3

    Fiscal year: together with document number and company code, it makes the document unique. The same number can exist again in 2027.

  • 4

    Document type: what kind of entry this is. SA is a plain GL account document. Others you will meet: KR (supplier invoice), AA (asset posting). The type controls the number range and which accounts are allowed.

  • 5

    Document date: the date on the original paper, here the landlord's invoice (25.02).

  • 6

    Posting date: the date the entry counts in the books. It decides the period. This is the date finance cares about.

  • 7

    Period: derived from the posting date and the fiscal year variant. 01.03 is period 3 (March). Period 3 must be open in OB52, or SAP refuses the posting.

  • 8

    Currency: Danish kroner. A euro invoice, for example from a reinsurer, would show EUR here and SAP would also store the DKK value.

  • 9

    Reference: the outside document's number, here the landlord's invoice number. Very useful when searching.

  • 10

    Entered by: the user who posted it (Maria). For automatic postings, such as FS-CD daily totals, you see a batch or interface user.

  • 11

    Posting key (PK): a two-digit code that says debit or credit and what kind of account. 40 = debit to a GL account, 50 = credit to a GL account. (For suppliers other keys are used, for example 31 for a supplier invoice.)

  • 12

    Account: which GL account the line goes to: 470000 Rent and 110000 Bank.

  • 13

    Amount: positive for debit, negative for credit. The lines add up to 0.00, so the document balances.

  • 14

    Cost centre: the CO part of the line. Rent is a cost, so SAP asks "which department?". Answer: 3000 Administration. The bank line has none, because a bank account is not a cost.

  • 15

    Text: a short note for humans. Good texts save hours of investigation later.

Document date vs posting date: the classic question

If the landlord's invoice (document date 25.02) were posted with posting date 28.02, the cost would count in February. Posted on 01.03, it counts in March. Same paper, different month. That choice is why month-end reports can surprise people.

5. The Universal Journal (ACDOCA)

In older SAP systems (SAP ECC), FI and CO kept their own tables, and finance spent days checking that they agreed. In S/4HANA there is one table for both: the Universal Journal, table ACDOCA. Every line item of every posting is one row in it, and that row carries both the FI view (company code, GL account, amount) and the CO view (cost centre, profit centre, order).

One table in the middle: legal reports and management reports read the same rows, so they cannot disagree. FS-CD still keeps the detail per policyholder; ACDOCA gets the totals.

Our rent document 100000017 becomes two rows in ACDOCA, roughly like this (simplified; the real table has hundreds of columns):

Simplified. A cost centre is linked to a profit centre in its master data, so SAP can fill the profit centre automatically. Premium postings carry the line of business (PC-MOTOR or PC-HOME) directly.

What this means for you as a BA: there is one version of the truth. When Jonas's cost centre report and Peter's P&L show different rent totals, the difference is in the selection (dates, accounts, cost centres), not in two separate ledgers.

6. A month at Nordica in five postings

Here are five simplified journal entries from March 2026. Each topic gets its own lesson later; for now just look at the debit and credit pattern. Rent is shown without VAT.

  1. 01.03, rent: Maria pays March rent from Danske Bank (the FB03 document above).
  2. 01.03, premium billing: FS-CD bills Hygge Hjem for policy ERH-2026-0042: premium 120,000 plus 1.1% insurance tax 1,320 = 121,320. FS-CD sends the total to the GL (Lesson 3). No VAT, because insurance is VAT-exempt.
  3. 04.03, telephony: the claims call-centre telephony bill is paid from the bank, cost centre 1000 Claims handling.
  4. 08.03, laptops: the invoice from Nordic IT Supply for 40 laptops for claims handlers arrives: 80,000 plus 25% VAT 20,000 = 100,000. Nordica cannot deduct the VAT, so all 100,000 is cost. Lesson 2 shows the real two steps (goods receipt, then invoice) through the GR/IR clearing account. Here they are combined into one line. Laptops are below the small-asset limit, so they are expensed (confirm the limit with your team).
  5. 31.03, depreciation: month-end posts one month of depreciation on the Valby office fit-out: 600,000 ÷ 10 years ÷ 12 months (Lesson 5).

Check it yourself

Debits: 50,000 + 121,320 + 18,000 + 100,000 + 5,000 = 294,320.
Credits: 50,000 + (120,000 + 1,320) + 18,000 + 100,000 + 5,000 = 294,320. Equal, as they must be.

Premium split: 120,000 + 1,320 = 121,320, which is the amount the customer owes. The 1,320 is not Nordica's income: it is a debt to Skattestyrelsen.

Bank: only postings 1 and 3 touch the bank, so it goes down by 50,000 + 18,000 = DKK 68,000. The premium has not been paid yet (Hygge Hjem pays on 13.03, Lesson 3), and the laptop invoice is not due until 07.04. Both sit as open items on 140000 and 160000.

Costs in these five postings only: 50,000 + 18,000 + 100,000 + 5,000 = DKK 173,000. This is not Nordica's March result. A full month has many more postings, and written premium is not the same as earned premium (Lesson 3).

7. What a FICO BA does, plus a BA toolkit

A FICO BA stands between finance people (Peter, Maria, Jonas), the actuaries and the SAP team. Typical work: write down how a business process should run in SAP, decide which accounts and cost objects each posting needs, write and run test cases, explain differences in reports, and help users during month-end. You do not need to be an accountant, but you must be able to read a journal entry and ask good questions. Reporting under IFRS 17, to Finanstilsynet and under Solvency II is owned by your actuarial and finance teams; you help make sure the right data reaches them.

7.1 Questions to ask

  • Which GL accounts does FS-CD post to, and at what level (daily totals, per line of business)?
  • Which company codes are in scope, and do they share a chart of accounts and a controlling area?
  • Is the fiscal year the calendar year? Who opens and closes posting periods, and when?
  • For each cost (rent, telephony, IT), which cost centre should carry it, and who is responsible for that cost centre?
  • Which result figures does management want: per line of business, product or channel?
  • Is VAT deductible on this purchase? (For most purchases at an insurer, no: the VAT is part of the cost.)
  • Which postings come automatically from other systems, and which are typed by hand? Which go through FS-CD and which through FI accounts payable?
  • Who owns the reserve estimates, and when do they arrive in SAP at month-end?
