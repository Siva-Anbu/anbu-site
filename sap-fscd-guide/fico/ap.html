Accounts payable: buying and paying at an insurer

Follow one order of laptops from the purchase order to the bank transfer, see which accounting entries each step creates, and learn why an insurer cannot reclaim VAT. About 20 minutes.

Before you start

This is Lesson 2 of the FICO course. It builds on Lesson 1: FICO basics, where you met company code NF01, GL accounts, debit and credit, and the Universal Journal. If "debit" and "credit" still feel strange, read that lesson first. It also helps to know that claims payouts live in the FSCD course (overview, FSCD basics, payments). All names, amounts and account numbers are made up for learning.

Meet the people in this story

Nordica Forsikring A/S in Copenhagen sells motor, home and travel insurance. In March 2026 the claims department hires new people, and they need computers. Over the next five weeks you, Sofie, will watch Nordica buy 40 laptops for claims handlers and pay for them. This chain is called procure to pay (P2P), and the finance part of it is accounts payable (AP): the money the company owes to its suppliers.

1. Procure to pay in one picture

Buying something in SAP is not one transaction. It is a chain of documents, each created by a different person, and each one pointing back to the one before. Purchasing and the warehouse work in MM (Materials Management, the logistics module). Finance works in FI (Financial Accounting). The interesting part for a BA is where the two meet: some MM steps create accounting entries automatically.

The P2P chain for PO 4500001234. Green "posting" means a journal entry lands in the Universal Journal (ACDOCA) automatically.

Why does the purchase order not create a posting? Because nothing has happened yet in the real world. Nordica has only promised to buy. Accounting records events that change what the company owns or owes: laptops arriving, a bill arriving, money leaving. The PO is still very important: every later step is checked against it.

2. Two roads for payments at an insurer

An insurer pays out money in many ways, and not all of it goes through accounts payable. This is the first thing a new BA at an insurer must get right. There are two roads:

Rule of thumb: if the money is a cost of running the company, it is AP. If it is part of the insurance business itself (claims, commission, premiums), it is FS-CD.

3. The supplier as a Business Partner

Before anyone can order from Nordic IT Supply, the supplier must exist in SAP. In S/4HANA every supplier and customer is a Business Partner (BP), maintained in transaction BP. The same BP can have several roles. Nordic IT Supply has the supplier role. A BP has general data (name, address, tax number, bank details) and company code data that is specific to NF01. For BP 300120:

Reconciliation account

In simple words: each supplier has its own small ledger (a sub-ledger) showing every invoice and payment. The general ledger does not list every supplier. It has one account, 160000, that always shows the total of all supplier sub-ledgers. You never post to 160000 directly; posting to a supplier updates it automatically.

Example: on 31.03 Nordica owes DKK 100,000 to Nordic IT Supply, DKK 12,500 to a marketing agency and DKK 6,250 to a cleaning company. FBL1N shows the three suppliers separately. GL account 160000 shows one number: DKK 118,750.

Details per supplier in the sub-ledger (FBL1N), one total in the general ledger (FS10N or FBL3N). The two always agree.

4. The insurance twist: VAT is a cost, not a refund

Most companies pay VAT (moms) on purchases and then deduct it in their VAT return: the tax agency gives it back. Danish insurance is VAT-exempt (momsfritaget). Nordica charges no VAT on premiums, so it also has no right to deduct the VAT on what it buys. The 25% VAT on the laptop invoice is simply part of what the laptops cost.

Financial companies pay lønsumsafgift (a payroll-based tax) instead of VAT. Always confirm the exact rules with your tax team; some costs can have special treatment.

For a BA this means two things in a test: (1) the tax code on Nordica's purchase invoices must be one that posts the VAT to the cost account, not to an input-VAT account, and (2) the cost centre must be filled on the VAT line too, because the VAT is a cost.

5. Goods receipt: the laptops arrive (05.03)

On Thursday 05.03 the laptops arrive. IT opens transaction MIGO, enters PO 4500001234, checks that 40 laptops came and posts the goods receipt. A material document and an accounting document are created. The value comes from the PO: 40 × DKK 2,000 = DKK 80,000. Without an invoice the system cannot credit the supplier yet, so it credits GR/IR clearing.

The laptops cost about DKK 2,500 each including VAT, which is below the small-asset limit, so they are expensed straight away, not capitalised and depreciated. They go to account 476000 (IT equipment, low value) with cost centre 1000 Claims handling. Confirm the limit with your finance team.

The goods receipt posting

Debit / CreditAccountCost centreDKK
Debit476000 IT equipment, low value100080,000.00
Credit191100 GR/IR clearing80,000.00

Nobody types these account numbers. MM account determination (configuration) decides them from the material and the movement type. That is why a BA checks it in testing: a wrong setting posts every delivery to the wrong account.

GR/IR clearing account

In simple words: a waiting room between the goods and the invoice. When goods arrive first, their value waits in GR/IR until the invoice comes. When the invoice arrives, the value leaves the waiting room and becomes a debt to the supplier. If everything matches, the room is empty again.

Example: from 05.03 to 07.03, DKK 80,000 sits in GR/IR. It means "we have the laptops, but Nordic IT Supply's bill hasn't arrived yet".

A GR/IR balance that never goes back to zero is a warning sign: goods without an invoice, or an invoice without goods.

6. The invoice and the three-way match (08.03)

Nordic IT Supply's invoice is dated Sunday 08.03: 40 × DKK 2,000 = DKK 80,000, plus 25% VAT DKK 20,000, total DKK 100,000. Maria enters it in MIRO (logistics invoice verification) with a reference to PO 4500001234. Before posting, SAP performs the three-way match.

Three-way match

In simple words: before you pay a bill, you check three papers against each other. What did we order (PO)? What did we receive (goods receipt)? What are we being charged (invoice)? Quantity and price must agree, within an agreed tolerance.

Example: PO says 40 at 2,000, the goods receipt says 40 laptops, the invoice says 40 at 2,000. All three agree, so the invoice is posted and free for payment.

The match happens at line level: each invoice line is compared with its PO line and the quantity received for that line.

The invoice posting

The invoice matches, so MIRO posts it. The waiting room empties, and because Nordica cannot deduct VAT, the DKK 20,000 VAT goes to the same cost account and cost centre as the laptops:

Debit / CreditAccountCost centreDKK
Debit191100 GR/IR clearing80,000.00
Debit476000 IT equipment (non-deductible VAT)100020,000.00
Credit160000 Trade payables (Nordic IT Supply, 300120)100,000.00
Total debit = total credit100,000.00

The supplier line creates an open item of DKK 100,000 on the supplier account, with a due date of 07.04 (invoice date 08.03 + 30 days). Note what is missing: there is no input VAT line. For a normal company there would be one; for an insurer the VAT is cost.

What if the price is wrong?

In simple words: if the difference is small, inside the tolerance, SAP accepts it. If it is bigger, the invoice is still posted, so the debt is in the books, but it gets a payment block. The payment run skips it until someone checks and releases it.

Example: suppose Nordic IT Supply had charged DKK 2,100 per laptop. Net DKK 84,000 instead of 80,000, a DKK 4,000 (5%) difference, far above a typical tolerance. The invoice is blocked. Purchasing either agrees the new price (and Maria releases the block in MRBR) or asks for a credit note.

Invoices without a purchase order: FB60

Not every bill has a PO. The cleaning company sends a monthly invoice of DKK 5,000 + 25% VAT DKK 1,250 = DKK 6,250. There was no order and no goods receipt, so there is nothing to match. Maria enters it directly in FI with FB60 and chooses the expense account herself, with cost centre 3000 Administration. Because VAT is not deductible, the full DKK 6,250 is cost. These invoices usually need an approval (someone confirms the service was delivered), because the three-way match cannot protect them.

7. Paying the supplier: the payment run (06.04)

Maria does not pay invoices one by one. Every Monday she runs the automatic payment program, F110. It looks at all open supplier items, picks the ones that must be paid now, creates the payment postings and writes a payment file for the bank.

The same idea as a dunning run in FSCD: a proposal you can check, then the real run.

StepWhat Maria doesFor our invoice
1. ParametersEnters a run date and an ID, the company code, payment methods, the next run date and which suppliers to includeRun date 06.04.2026, company code NF01, method bank transfer, next run 13.04
2. ProposalSAP lists every open item that is due before the next run, grouped per supplierDue 07.04, which is before 13.04. If it waits a week it would be late, so it is picked today
3. Review / editChecks the list; can block an item or change the bank usedMaria sees DKK 100,000 to Nordic IT Supply, no block. OK

5. Payment mediumCreates the bank file and (optionally) a payment advice to the supplierA pain.001 file goes to Danske Bank, which transfers the money

The payment posting

Debit / CreditAccountDKK
Debit160000 Trade payables (Nordic IT Supply, 300120)100,000.00
Credit110000 Bank: Danske Bank100,000.00

The debit on the supplier clears the open item: the invoice and the payment are linked by a clearing document and both become cleared items. Many companies credit an outgoing payments clearing account instead of the bank account itself, and move it to the bank account when the bank statement confirms the money left. We keep it simple and credit 110000.

ISO 20022 pain.001

In simple words: a standard XML file format for "please make these payments". SAP writes the file, and the bank reads it. The same format is used by banks all over Europe, so one setup can talk to many banks.

Example: the 06.04 file contains one payment: DKK 100,000 to the supplier's IBAN, with the invoice number as the reference so Nordic IT Supply knows what it is for.

Danish note: how suppliers want to be paid

Most Danish B2B payments are plain bank transfers. Many Danish bills also carry an FI-kort payment line (types +71, +73 or +75): a creditor number plus, for +71 and +75, a payment reference that tells the supplier which invoice is paid. When a supplier invoice has an FI-kort, AP enters that information so the payment run pays it with the right reference. Ask early which payment methods the bank and suppliers expect.

The whole story on one timeline

  1. 02.03.2026: purchasing creates PO 4500001234 for 40 laptops at DKK 2,000 = DKK 80,000 net. No posting.
  2. 05.03: goods receipt in MIGO. IT equipment cost +80,000 (cost centre 1000), GR/IR 80,000 waiting.
  3. 08.03: invoice dated 08.03, DKK 100,000 incl. VAT, posted in MIRO. GR/IR back to 0, VAT of 20,000 booked as cost, supplier open item DKK 100,000, due 07.04.
  4. 06.04: payment run F110 (next run 13.04). The item is due before the next run, so it is paid. Payment document posted, open item cleared, pain.001 file to Danske Bank.
  5. 07.04: the due date. Nordic IT Supply already has its money.

In plain words: Nordica gave away DKK 100,000 from the bank. In return the claims department has laptops, and the full DKK 100,000 is a cost of cost centre 1000. There is no VAT refund. GR/IR is empty and nobody owes anybody anything. These DKK 100,000 are also part of the "laptops 100,000" in March's operating expenses, which you will meet again in Lesson 4.

A BA spends much of their time on the exceptions. These are the ones every AP team knows.

Cash discount (early payment discount)

In simple words: some suppliers say "pay early and pay less". Terms like 2% within 8 days, 30 days net mean: pay within 8 days and take 2% off, or pay the full amount within 30 days. F110 can pick such items early to use the discount, if the run settings allow it.

Example (not our real terms, which are 30 days net): with 2/8 terms, paying Nordic IT Supply by 16.03 would cost DKK 100,000 − 2% = DKK 98,000, saving DKK 2,000. Since all of the invoice is cost for Nordica, the saving reduces cost, either on a discount account or on the cost account, depending on settings.

10. BA toolkit for accounts payable

10.1 Questions to ask

  • Which purchases must have a PO, and which may be entered with FB60? Who approves non-PO invoices?
  • Which tax codes are used on purchases, and do they all post VAT to cost? Who in the tax team confirms this, including lønsumsafgift?
  • What is the small-asset limit, and which spend is expensed versus capitalised?
  • What price and quantity tolerances apply? Is invoice verification GR-based?
  • Which payments are AP (suppliers) and which are FS-CD (claims, commission)? Where is the border for loss adjusters, repairers and brokers?
  • How often does the payment run happen, which banks and payment methods are used, and who reviews the proposal?
  • Who may create suppliers and change bank details? Is dual control switched on?
  • How do invoices arrive: paper, PDF by email, or electronic (OIOUBL / Peppol)? Is there scanning or OCR?

A closing tip

When someone says "AP is wrong", ask for the PO number first. With ME23N you can see the order, the goods receipt and the invoice in one history, and from there jump to every accounting document. And always ask the first insurance question: is this really an AP payment, or does it belong to claims and FS-CD?
