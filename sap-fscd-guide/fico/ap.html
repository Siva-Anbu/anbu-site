Accounts payable: from purchase to payment

Follow one load of oak from the order to the bank transfer: the purchase order, the goods receipt, the supplier invoice and the payment run, and the accounting entries each step creates. About 20 minutes.

Before you start

This is Lesson 2 of the FICO course. It builds on Lesson 1: FICO basics, where you met company code DK01, GL accounts, debit and credit, and the Universal Journal. If "debit" and "credit" still feel strange, read section 3 of Lesson 1 again first. All names, amounts and account numbers are made up for learning.

Meet the people in this story

Nordlys Møbler A/S in Aarhus makes oak dining tables. Tables need wood, and in March 2026 the workshop is running low. Over the next five weeks you, Sofie, will watch the company buy 20 m³ of oak from its regular supplier and pay for it. This whole chain is called procure to pay (P2P), and the finance part of it is accounts payable (AP): the money the company owes to its suppliers.

1. Procure to pay in one picture

Buying something in SAP is not one transaction. It is a chain of documents, each created by a different person, and each one pointing back to the one before. Purchasing and the warehouse work in MM (Materials Management, the logistics module). Finance works in FI (Financial Accounting). The interesting part for a BA is where the two meet: some MM steps create accounting entries automatically.

The P2P chain for PO 4500001234. Green "posting" means a journal entry lands in the Universal Journal (ACDOCA) automatically.

Why does the purchase order not create a posting? Because nothing has happened yet in the real world. Nordlys has only promised to buy. Accounting records events that change what the company owns or owes: wood arriving, a bill arriving, money leaving. The PO is still very important: every later step is checked against it.

2. The supplier as a Business Partner

Before anyone can order from Jysk Træ, the supplier must exist in SAP. In S/4HANA every supplier and customer is a Business Partner (BP), maintained in transaction BP. The same BP can have several roles: Jysk Træ has the supplier role. If it ever bought furniture from Nordlys too, it could get the customer role on the same BP number.

A BP has general data (name, address, tax number, bank details) that is the same everywhere, and company code data that is specific to DK01. For Jysk Træ, BP 300120:

FieldValue for Jysk TræWhy it matters
Name, addressJysk Træ ApS, SilkeborgPrinted on the payment advice and the bank file
VAT numberDK + its 8-digit CVR numberIn Denmark the CVR number is the company registration number; the VAT number is "DK" plus the CVR number
Bank detailsAccount at its bank (IBAN)Where the payment run sends the money. A wrong digit here means paying the wrong person
Reconciliation account160000 Trade payablesThe GL account that collects the totals of all suppliers (explained below)
Payment terms30 days netPay the full amount within 30 days of the invoice date
Payment methodsBank transferHow the payment run is allowed to pay this supplier

Reconciliation account

In simple words: each supplier has its own small ledger (a sub-ledger) showing every invoice and payment. The general ledger does not list every supplier. It has one account, 160000, that always shows the total of all supplier sub-ledgers. You never post to 160000 directly; posting to a supplier updates it automatically.

Example: on 31.03 Nordlys owes DKK 100,000 to Jysk Træ, DKK 12,500 to a glue supplier and DKK 6,250 to a cleaning company. FBL1N shows the three suppliers separately. GL account 160000 shows one number: DKK 118,750.

Details per supplier in the sub-ledger (FBL1N), one total in the general ledger (FS10N or FBL3N). The two always agree.

3. Goods receipt: the oak arrives (05.03)

On Thursday 05.03 a lorry from Silkeborg delivers the oak. The warehouse worker opens transaction MIGO, enters PO 4500001234, checks that 20 m³ arrived and posts the goods receipt. Two documents are created: a material document (the logistics record: "20 m³ in stock") and an accounting document, because Nordlys now owns wood worth money.

The value comes from the PO: 20 m³ × DKK 4,000 = DKK 80,000. But there is no invoice yet, so the system cannot credit Jysk Træ. Instead it credits a special account: GR/IR clearing.

The goods receipt posting

Debit / CreditAccountDKK
Debit200000 Raw materials: oak timber80,000.00
Credit191100 GR/IR clearing80,000.00

Nobody types these account numbers. MM account determination (configuration) decides them from the material and the movement type. That is why the BA must check this configuration in testing: a wrong setting posts every delivery to the wrong account.

GR/IR clearing account

In simple words: a waiting room between the goods and the invoice. When goods arrive first, their value waits in GR/IR until the invoice comes. When the invoice arrives, the value leaves the waiting room and becomes a debt to the supplier. If everything matches, the room is empty again.

Example: from 05.03 to 08.03, DKK 80,000 sits in GR/IR. It means "we have the oak, but Jysk Træ's bill hasn't arrived yet".

A GR/IR balance that never goes back to zero is a warning sign: goods without an invoice, or an invoice without goods.

4. The invoice and the three-way match (08.03)

Jysk Træ's invoice is dated Sunday 08.03: 20 m³ × DKK 4,000 = DKK 80,000, plus 25% Danish VAT (moms) DKK 20,000, total DKK 100,000. Maria enters it in MIRO (logistics invoice verification), referring to PO 4500001234. MIRO proposes the PO lines and the received quantity, and Maria enters the invoice total and VAT code. Before posting, SAP performs the three-way match.

Three-way match

In simple words: before you pay a bill, you check three papers against each other. What did we order (PO)? What did we receive (goods receipt)? What are we being charged (invoice)? Quantity and price must agree, within an agreed tolerance.

Example: PO says 20 m³ at 4,000, the goods receipt says 20 m³, the invoice says 20 m³ at 4,000. All three agree, so the invoice is posted and free for payment.

The match happens at line level: each invoice line is compared with its PO line and the quantity received for that line.

The invoice posting

The invoice matches, so MIRO posts it. Look at what happens to the waiting room:

Debit / CreditAccountDKK
Debit191100 GR/IR clearing80,000.00
Debit154000 Input VAT (købsmoms)20,000.00
Credit160000 Trade payables (supplier Jysk Træ, 300120)100,000.00
Total debit = total credit100,000.00

The DKK 80,000 leaves GR/IR. The supplier line creates an open item of DKK 100,000 on Jysk Træ's account, with a due date of 07.04 (invoice date 08.03 + 30 days). The VAT is an asset: Nordlys will get it back from the Danish tax agency (Skattestyrelsen) in its VAT return, because it is a VAT-registered business.

What if the price is wrong?

In simple words: if the difference is small, inside the tolerance, SAP accepts it. If it is bigger, the invoice is still posted, so the debt is in the books, but it gets a payment block. The payment run skips it until someone checks and releases it.

Example: suppose Jysk Træ had charged DKK 4,200 per m³. Net DKK 84,000 instead of 80,000, a DKK 4,000 (5%) difference. That is far above a typical tolerance, so the invoice is blocked. Purchasing either agrees the new price (and Maria releases the block in MRBR) or asks Jysk Træ for a credit note.

Invoices without a purchase order: FB60

Not every bill has a PO. The cleaning company sends a monthly invoice of DKK 5,000 + 25% VAT = DKK 6,250. There was no order and no goods receipt, so there is nothing to match. Maria enters it directly in FI with FB60 (enter supplier invoice) and chooses the expense account herself, for example a cleaning expense account with cost centre 3000 Administration. These invoices usually need an approval (someone confirms the service was delivered) because the three-way match cannot protect them.

5. Paying the supplier: the payment run (06.04)

Maria does not pay invoices one by one. Every Monday she runs the automatic payment program, F110. It looks at all open supplier items, picks the ones that must be paid now, creates the payment postings and writes a payment file for the bank.

The same idea as a dunning run: a proposal you can check, then the real run.

StepWhat Maria doesFor our invoice
1. ParametersEnters a run date and an ID, the company code, payment methods, the next run date and which suppliers to includeRun date 06.04.2026, company code DK01, method bank transfer, next run 13.04
2. ProposalSAP lists every open item that is due before the next run, grouped per supplierDue 07.04, and 07.04 is before 13.04. If it waits a week it would be late, so it is picked today
3. Review / editChecks the list; can block an item or change the bank usedMaria sees DKK 100,000 to Jysk Træ, no block. OK
4. Payment runPosts one payment document per supplier and clears the paid itemsPayment document for DKK 100,000; the invoice is cleared
5. Payment mediumCreates the bank file and (optionally) a payment advice to the supplierA pain.001 file goes to Danske Bank, which transfers the money

The payment posting

Debit / CreditAccountDKK
Debit160000 Trade payables (supplier Jysk Træ, 300120)100,000.00
Credit110000 Bank: Danske Bank100,000.00

The debit on the supplier clears the open item: the invoice and the payment are linked by a clearing document and both become cleared items. Many companies credit an outgoing payments clearing account instead of the bank account itself, and move it to the bank account only when the bank statement confirms the money left. We keep it simple here and credit 110000.

ISO 20022 pain.001

In simple words: a standard XML file format for "please make these payments". SAP writes the file, and the bank reads it. The same format is used by banks all over Europe, so one setup can talk to many banks.

Example: the 06.04 file contains one payment: DKK 100,000 to Jysk Træ's IBAN, with the invoice number as the reference so Jysk Træ knows what it is for.

Danish note: how suppliers want to be paid

Most Danish B2B payments are plain bank transfers to a bank account. Many Danish bills also carry an FI-kort payment line (types +71, +73 or +75): a creditor number plus, for +71 and +75, a payment reference that tells the supplier exactly which invoice is paid. When a supplier invoice has an FI-kort, the AP team enters that information so the payment run can pay it with the right reference. Ask early in a Danish project which payment methods the bank and suppliers expect.

The whole story on one timeline

  1. 02.03.2026: purchasing creates PO 4500001234 for 20 m³ oak at DKK 4,000 = DKK 80,000 net. No posting.
  2. 05.03: goods receipt in MIGO. Raw materials +80,000, GR/IR 80,000 waiting.
  3. 08.03: invoice dated 08.03, DKK 100,000 incl. VAT, posted in MIRO. GR/IR back to 0, Jysk Træ open item DKK 100,000, due 07.04.
  4. 06.04: payment run F110 (next run 13.04). The item is due before the next run, so it is paid. Payment document posted, open item cleared, pain.001 file to Danske Bank.
  5. 07.04: the due date. Jysk Træ already has its money. Nobody is late, nobody is early by much.

7. Things that go wrong

A BA spends much of their time on the exceptions. These are the ones every AP team knows.

Cash discount (early payment discount)

In simple words: some suppliers say "pay early and pay less". Payment terms like 2% within 8 days, 30 days net mean: pay within 8 days and take 2% off, or pay the full amount within 30 days. F110 can pick such items early to use the discount, if the run settings allow it.

Example (not our real terms, which are 30 days net): with 2/8 terms, paying by 16.03 would cost DKK 100,000 − 2% = DKK 98,000, saving DKK 2,000. SAP posts the discount to a discount account and, depending on settings, also corrects the VAT part.

8. BA toolkit for accounts payable

8.1 Questions to ask

  • Which purchases must have a PO, and which may be entered with FB60? Who approves non-PO invoices?
  • What price and quantity tolerances apply? Are they the same for all suppliers and materials?
  • Is invoice verification GR-based (invoice only after goods receipt)?
  • Which fields does the duplicate check use? Should it warn or stop?
  • How often does the payment run happen, which payment methods and banks are used, and who reviews the proposal?
  • Who may create suppliers and change bank details? Is dual control switched on?
  • How do invoices arrive: paper, PDF by email, or electronic (OIOUBL / Peppol in Denmark)? Is there OCR or a scanning tool?
  • Who looks at old GR/IR balances at month-end, and how often?

A closing tip

When someone says "AP is wrong", ask for the PO number first. With ME23N you can see the order, the goods receipt and the invoice in one history, and from there jump to every accounting document. Nine out of ten P2P questions are answered by that one screen.
