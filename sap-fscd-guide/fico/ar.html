Accounts receivable: from sale to cash

Nordlys Møbler sells 10 oak tables to Hygge Hjem A/S. Follow the money from the sales order to the bank statement, and learn what SAP posts at each step, what happens when a customer pays too little or not at all, and how to check it yourself.

Now you follow money coming in from a customer. Accounts receivable (AR, in SAP called FI-AR) is the part of finance that tracks what customers owe the company and collects it.

1. Order to cash in one picture

Order to cash (O2C) is the whole journey from a customer's order to the money in the bank. Most of the steps happen in SD, the sales module. Only some of them create a posting in FI, the finance module. A good BA knows exactly which ones.

The sales order only promises. Goods issue moves stock value, billing creates the receivable, and the payment clears it.

StepPosts to FI?What changes in the books
Sales order (VA01)NoNothing. It is a promise to deliver. It may reserve stock and check the credit limit.
Goods issue (VL01N, then post goods issue)YesDebit cost of goods sold, credit finished goods inventory, at the tables' production cost. The tables are gone, so their value leaves stock.
Billing (VF01)YesReceivable from Hygge Hjem, sales revenue and output VAT (section 3).
Incoming paymentYesMoney in the bank, receivable cleared (section 4).

In S/4HANA all these postings land in the same table, the Universal Journal (ACDOCA), so finance and controlling see the same numbers. The sales order, delivery and invoice are linked in a document flow, which you can open from VA03 or VF03 to see every step and its status.

2. The customer as a Business Partner

In S/4HANA a customer is a Business Partner (BP), maintained with transaction BP. One BP can play several roles: the role "FI customer" holds the finance data, the role "customer" holds the sales data. Hygge Hjem is BP 100250 with both roles. Here is what finance cares about:

Reconciliation account

In simple words: the GL account that collects the total of all customer balances. You never post to it directly; every customer posting updates it automatically.

Example: Hygge Hjem's reconciliation account is 140000 Trade receivables. When the invoice posts to the customer, 140000 goes up by the same DKK 150,000.00. The balance sheet shows 140000; AR shows who owes what.

Payment terms

In simple words: a key that tells SAP when the invoice is due, and whether there is a discount for paying early.

Example: 14 days net. Invoice dated 15.03 + 14 days = due 29.03.

Credit limit

In simple words: the most the customer may owe at any one time. If a new order would push them over it, the order is blocked until someone approves it.

Example: Hygge Hjem has a limit of DKK 500,000.00. With nothing else open, the DKK 150,000.00 order passes. In S/4HANA this lives in SAP Credit Management (screen UKM_BP), which can also give each customer a risk class and a score.

Dunning procedure

In simple words: the rulebook for payment reminders: how many levels, how many days apart, and any fees (section 6).

VAT and CVR number

In simple words: the CVR number is the Danish company registration number. It is stored on the BP and printed on invoices, and it matters for VAT reporting.

Example: Hygge Hjem is a Danish company, so the invoice carries 25% Danish VAT (moms). A customer in Germany would usually get an invoice without Danish VAT (reverse charge), which needs a different tax code.

3. Billing and the FI posting (15.03)

On 15.03 the billing clerk creates the invoice in SD with VF01. When the billing document is saved and released to accounting, SAP creates the accounting document on its own. Nobody types a journal entry.

LineDebit / CreditAccountDKK
1DebitCustomer 100250 Hygge Hjem (reconciliation account 140000 Trade receivables)150,000.00
2Credit800000 Sales revenue: domestic120,000.00
3Credit175000 Output VAT (salgsmoms), tax code for 25% output VAT30,000.00

Accounting document dated 15.03.2026, document type RV (billing transfer) in many systems. Debits 150,000.00 = credits 120,000.00 + 30,000.00.

How does SAP know to use 800000 and 175000? Through account determination. The SD invoice is built from condition types: small price building blocks such as "base price" and "output tax". Consultants set up rules that map each condition type to a GL account. The customer line goes to the reconciliation account from the BP.

Revenue account determination can depend on sales organisation, customer group and material group. A BA confirms the business rule; a consultant builds it.

BA tip: "the invoice did not reach accounting"

If a billing document exists but no accounting document, it is often blocked for transfer, usually because an account could not be determined. The billing clerk sees an error such as "account determination error". Ask for the condition type and the missing rule, not just "it failed".

4. Incoming payment (27.03)

Danish businesses often pay with an FI-kort (in English: a payment slip). Type +71 carries a 15-digit payment ID that Nordlys Møbler prints on its invoice. Hygge Hjem types it into their bank when they pay, and the bank passes it back to Nordlys Møbler on the bank statement. That is what makes automatic matching possible.

Each morning, Danske Bank sends the electronic bank statement for the day before, as a camt.053 file (the modern XML format) or an older MT940 file. Finance loads it with FF_5, often as a scheduled job. For every line, SAP reads the reference, looks for the matching open item and, if it finds one, posts and clears in one go.

The rules that read references from the statement are called search strings and interpretation algorithms. Their quality decides how much manual work finance has.

Hygge Hjem's DKK 150,000.00 arrives on 27.03 with the right payment ID. SAP posts two small entries through a clearing account, a "waiting room" account that should always end at zero:

Date · stepDebitCreditDKK
27.03 · bank side110000 Bank: Danske Bank113100 Bank incoming clearing150,000.00
27.03 · customer side113100 Bank incoming clearingCustomer 100250 (140000 Trade receivables)150,000.00

The second posting also clears the invoice: the open item and the payment get the same clearing document and date, and the item moves from "open" to "cleared".

If there were no reference, the first line would still post (the money is in the bank), but the customer side would wait in FEBAN. 113100 would show a balance until someone matches it. That makes 113100 a handy health check: a balance that grows means unmatched payments.

Manual incoming payment: F-28

In simple words: a screen to post a customer payment by hand, choose which open items it pays, and clear them.

Example: if Hygge Hjem had brought a cheque or the bank file had failed, a clerk would use F-28: bank account 110000, amount 150,000.00, customer 100250, then select the open invoice. Many projects use the Fiori app "Post incoming payments" instead.

5. When the amount doesn't match

Real customers do not always pay the exact amount. SAP has a standard answer for each case. Here they are, using the same DKK 150,000.00 invoice.

5.1 Partial payment or residual item

Either way the customer still owes DKK 50,000.00. The choice changes what the open-item list and dunning letters show.

5.2 Short payments, overpayments and tolerances

CaseExampleWhat SAP usually does
Tiny short paymentPays 149,950.00 (bank fee deducted)Inside the payment difference tolerance, so the 50.00 is written off automatically to an expense account and the invoice is cleared.
Bigger short paymentPays 147,000.00 without explanationOutside tolerance. The clerk posts a residual item of 3,000.00 with a reason code such as "price dispute", so someone follows up.
OverpaymentPays 151,000.00Invoice cleared, 1,000.00 stays as a credit on the customer's account. It can be paid back or used against the next invoice.

Tolerance amounts are set per company code and can be limited per user, so a clerk cannot write off large amounts.

5.3 Cash discount

Imagine the terms had been "2% within 8 days, otherwise 14 days net". The discount is 2% of the gross amount: 2% × 150,000.00 = 3,000.00. If Hygge Hjem paid by 23.03, they could pay DKK 147,000.00 and the invoice would still be fully cleared. Because the price really went down, the VAT goes down too, so in Denmark the discount is usually split between net and VAT:

Debit / CreditAccountDKK
Debit113100 Bank incoming clearing (the money received)147,000.00
DebitCash discount granted (expense account set by your project)2,400.00
Debit175000 Output VAT (VAT on the discount: 3,000 × 20%)600.00
CreditCustomer 100250 (140000 Trade receivables)150,000.00

147,000 + 2,400 + 600 = 150,000. The VAT share of a gross amount at 25% VAT is 20% (25/125). Whether VAT is adjusted depends on the tax code setup; confirm with your tax team.

In our story the terms are 14 days net, so there is no discount and Hygge Hjem pays the full 150,000.00.

5.4 Credit memos

A credit memo is a "negative invoice". Suppose one table arrived scratched and Nordlys Møbler agrees to take DKK 12,000.00 net off. Sales creates a credit memo (from a credit memo request in SD), and FI posts the reverse of the invoice: debit revenue 12,000.00 (or a sales deductions account), debit output VAT 3,000.00, credit the customer 15,000.00. Hygge Hjem would then owe 150,000 − 15,000 = DKK 135,000.00.

6. When customers don't pay

In our story Hygge Hjem pays two days early. But suppose they had not. Dunning is the process of sending payment reminders. In SAP the dunning run (F150) works in two steps: a proposal lists who would get which reminder, someone checks it, and then the print run sends the letters and records the new dunning level on each item.

An example ladder with at least 10 days between letters. Your project's days, texts and fees will differ.

6.1 Danish rules to check

Reminders and interest are regulated by the Danish Interest Act (renteloven). Rules change, so treat these as points to confirm with your legal or finance team, not as legal advice:

  • Reminder fees are capped (DKK 100 per reminder at the time of writing), only a limited number of fee-bearing reminders are allowed, and there must be at least 10 days between them.
  • Business to business: when a business customer pays late, the creditor may be entitled to a fixed compensation of DKK 310 for collection costs. Check how this combines with reminder fees in your company's policy.
  • Interest on late payment is usually allowed from the due date between businesses, at a statutory rate (the central bank reference rate plus a fixed margin) unless the contract says otherwise. SAP can calculate it with an interest run or as part of dunning.

6.2 Bad debt: provision and write-off

If a debt looks doubtful, finance books a provision: an expense now, for money that probably won't come, while the item stays open. If the customer goes bankrupt and the money is really lost, the item is written off: the receivable is cleared against a bad debt expense account. Output VAT on a confirmed loss can usually be reclaimed from SKAT (the Danish tax agency); confirm the rules with your tax team.

7.1 Ageing and DSO

An ageing report sorts open items by how many days they are overdue. Peter Lund looks at it every month: money in the 90+ bucket is at risk.

Bucket (days overdue)What it usually means
Not yet dueNormal. Hygge Hjem's invoice sat here from 15.03 to 29.03.
0–30A little late. Level 1 reminder.
31–60Worth a phone call. Level 2 or 3.
61–90Serious. Credit block, talk of collection.
90+Probably needs a provision or collection agency.

DSO (days sales outstanding) says how many days of sales are waiting to be paid on average: receivables ÷ sales × number of days. Example: receivables of DKK 300,000.00 at the end of a quarter, sales (incl. VAT) of DKK 1,350,000.00 in that 90-day quarter: 300,000 ÷ 1,350,000 × 90 = 20 days. Lower is better.

8. BA toolkit for accounts receivable

8.1 Questions to ask

  • Which payment methods do customers use (FI-kort +71, bank transfer, direct debit), and which reference do they send?
  • What share of incoming payments should clear automatically? Who works the FEBAN queue, and by when?
  • What are the payment difference tolerances, per company and per user? Which reason codes do we need?
  • Partial payment or residual item: which does finance prefer, and why?
  • Who sets and approves credit limits? What happens to an order that is blocked?
  • What does each dunning level say, after how many days, with which fee, and does legal approve the texts?
  • When is a debt provisioned, and who approves a write-off?

A closing tip

Watch the bank incoming clearing account (113100). If it is zero at the end of each day, payments are matching well. If it keeps growing, ask why: it is often missing references on invoices, or a search string that needs fixing.
