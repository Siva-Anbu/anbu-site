Premiums and receivables: from policy to cash

Nordica Forsikring sells a commercial property policy to Hygge Hjem A/S. Follow the premium from the policy system to the bank statement, see what FS-CD and the general ledger each post, and learn how written premium turns into earned premium month by month.

Before you start

This is Lesson 3 of the FICO course. It builds on Lesson 1: FICO basics (company code, GL accounts, debit and credit) and mirrors Lesson 2: Accounts payable, where Nordica was the one paying. The premium collection itself is explained in the FSCD course (basics, payments, dunning, accounting). Here we look at it from the finance side. About a 20-minute read. All names, amounts and numbers are made up for learning.

You are Sofie Holm, a business analyst in the finance team of Nordica Forsikring A/S in Copenhagen, a Danish non-life insurer (company code NF01, currency DKK). An insurer does not sell goods, so there are no sales orders or deliveries. It sells cover, and customers pay a premium for it. Receivables are the premiums that have been billed but not yet paid.

1. Premium to cash in one picture

At an insurer the journey from policy to cash is shared between three systems. A BA must know who owns what, otherwise every question ends in "ask someone else".

The policy system sells and prices. FS-CD keeps one account per policyholder. FI only sees the totals.

SystemOwnsExample
Policy systemPolicy, cover period, premium amountERH-2026-0042, 120,000 for 12 months
FS-CDBilling, collection, dunning, one balance per policyholderOpen item of 121,320 for Hygge Hjem

In S/4HANA the FI postings land in the Universal Journal (ACDOCA), so finance and controlling share the same numbers. Details of FS-CD are in the FSCD course; this lesson stays at the FI side.

2. The policyholder as Business Partner and the contract account

In S/4HANA a customer is a Business Partner (BP), maintained with transaction BP. Hygge Hjem is BP 100250. In FS-CD the BP has a contract account, the "wallet" where all its premiums, payments and fees live.

Reconciliation account

In simple words: the GL account that holds the total of all FS-CD balances. Nobody posts to it by hand.

Example: 140000 Premium receivables from policyholders. If Nordica has 8,000 policyholders with open premiums, the GL shows one number and FS-CD shows who owes what. FPL9 shows one policyholder's balance.

VAT and insurance tax

In simple words: insurance is VAT-exempt in Denmark, so there is no VAT on premiums. Instead the premium carries insurance tax (skadesforsikringsafgift), which Nordica collects for the state.

Example: this course uses a simplified 1.1%: 1.1% of 120,000.00 = 1,320.00. Nordica pays it on to Skattestyrelsen. Motor liability has its own separate tax. Confirm current rates with your tax team.

Payment terms and dunning procedure

In simple words: terms say when the premium is due. The dunning procedure says which reminders follow if it is not paid (section 6).

Example: billed 01.03, due 15.03.

3. Billing and the GL posting (01.03)

On 01.03 FS-CD runs billing for the policy. It creates a posting document in the sub-ledger. Nothing is typed into FI: the nightly transfer to the general ledger (FPG1 in many projects) sends the daily totals.

LineDebit / CreditAccountDKK
1Debit140000 Premium receivables (policyholder 100250 in FS-CD)121,320.00
2Credit300000 Gross written premium120,000.00
3Credit290100 Insurance tax payable1,320.00

Debit 121,320.00 = credits 120,000.00 + 1,320.00. In the real GL, thousands of such policies are summed into one line per account and day.

How does the GL know where to post? Each FS-CD posting has a main transaction (what it is, for example "premium") and a sub transaction (more detail, for example "commercial property"). Account determination maps the pair to GL accounts. Consultants set it up; the BA confirms the business rule.

Different lines of business (motor, home) can post to different premium accounts or profit centres. That is a business rule, not a technical detail.

BA tip: "FS-CD says billed, the GL does not"

If an FS-CD document exists but the GL total is missing it, the transfer job may not have run or the account determination may be incomplete. Ask for the main and sub transaction and the posting date, not just "it failed".

4. Payment (13.03)

Danish customers often pay with an FI-kort (payment slip). The +71 type carries a payment ID that Nordica prints on the premium notice. Hygge Hjem types it into the bank, and the bank returns it on the statement. That lets FS-CD find the open item automatically.

Each morning Danske Bank sends the bank statement as a camt.053 file (XML) or an older MT940 file. The statement is loaded as a payment lot (FP05 in many projects). For every line FS-CD reads the reference, looks for the open item and, if found, posts and clears it.

Payments that cannot be matched wait on a clarification list. Until then the money is in the bank but the policyholder still looks unpaid.

Hygge Hjem's DKK 121,320.00 arrives on 13.03 with the right payment ID. FS-CD clears the open item. In the GL the totals appear through a clearing account, a "waiting room" account that should end at zero:

Date · stepDebitCreditDKK
13.03 · bank side110000 Bank: Danske Bank113100 Bank incoming clearing121,320.00
13.03 · policyholder side113100 Bank incoming clearing140000 Premium receivables (100250)121,320.00

The open premium and the payment get the same clearing document and date. Payment two days before the due date means no reminder is ever created.

If a statement line has no usable reference, the bank side still posts, but the policyholder side waits for clarification, so 113100 keeps a balance. A growing 113100 is a handy health check: it means unmatched payments.

5. Written premium, earned premium and the UPR

Here is the idea that surprises most new BAs. Hygge Hjem paid for 12 months of cover on 13.03, but Nordica has only provided one month of it by 31.03. Accounting says income belongs to the period in which the cover is given. So we split the premium:

Written premium and earned premium

In simple words: Written premium (GWP) is what was billed for the whole policy. Earned premium is the part of it for cover already given.

Example: policy ERH-2026-0042 is written at 120,000 on 01.03. After one month, 120,000 ÷ 12 = 10,000 is earned.

Unearned premium reserve (UPR, præmiehensættelser)

In simple words: a liability for cover Nordica still owes. If the policy were cancelled tomorrow, part of the premium would go back.

Example: at 31.03, 110,000 relates to 11 months not yet given, so it sits in 250000 UPR.

Simple pro-rata (one twelfth a month). Real systems may earn by day. Your actuarial team owns the method.

At month-end, finance books the change in UPR so that only earned premium reaches the result:

Date · stepDebitCreditDKK
31.03 · reserve the unearned part300500 Change in unearned premium reserve250000 Unearned premium reserve (UPR)110,000.00

Premium result for this policy: 120,000 written − 110,000 change in UPR = 10,000 earned.

6. When premiums are not paid

If Hygge Hjem had not paid by 15.03, dunning in FS-CD would start. This is the same process as in the dunning lesson. In short, reminders go out in steps, each firmer than the last, and the final one warns that cover will stop.

An example ladder with at least 10 days between letters. Your project's days, texts and fees will differ.

6.1 Cancellation and insurance tax

If the policyholder still does not pay after the final notice, the policy can be cancelled for non-payment, if the letter followed the legal rules (see the dunning lesson). The unpaid premium that was already billed for the period after the cancellation date is reversed: the written premium falls and so does the receivable. The insurance tax on the reversed part is reversed too, so Nordica does not owe Skattestyrelsen tax on premium it never collects. Confirm the exact treatment with your tax team.

6.2 Bad debt provision

Some premium for cover already given may never be paid. Finance books a provision (expense now, for money that probably will not come) based on age. If the debt is certain to be lost, FS-CD writes it off (FP04 in many projects) against a bad debt expense account. Ask your finance team for the ageing rules, for example "provision 50% over 90 days".

8. BA toolkit for premium receivables

8.1 Questions to ask

  • Which parts are in the policy system, which in FS-CD, and which in FI? Who owns the interface?
  • When does the FS-CD to GL transfer run, and what happens if it fails?
  • Which tax rate and tax account apply per product? Where does motor liability tax go?
  • How is premium earned (monthly, daily)? Who approves the UPR method?
  • What share of payments should match automatically? Who works the clarification list?
  • When do we cancel for non-payment, and how are written premium and tax reversed?
  • When is a premium debt provisioned or written off, and who approves it?
  • Which receivables are not premiums (reinsurers, recourse) and who chases them?

A closing tip

Reconcile FS-CD with the GL every day: the FS-CD balance of all policyholders should equal the balance of 140000. If they differ, the transfer job or account determination is the first place to look.
