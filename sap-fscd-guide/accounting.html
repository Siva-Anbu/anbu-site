From FSCD to the general ledger

How every FSCD posting turns into accounting: account determination, journal entries, reconciliation keys, the GL transfer, reconciliation and month-end. Explained with Mette's year.

Before you start

This lesson builds on Lesson 1 (posting documents, main and sub transactions, reconciliation keys) and Lesson 3 (fees and write-offs). You don't need an accounting background: debit and credit are explained as we go. All account numbers are made up for learning.

Why finance cares about FSCD

Finance doesn't look at Mette's account. They look at the general ledger (GL): total premium income, total money owed by customers, total claims paid. Every one of those numbers is built from millions of small FSCD postings. If the link between FSCD and the GL is wrong, the company's accounts are wrong. That's why finance teams ask BAs questions like "which GL account does this new fee post to?" and "why doesn't FSCD match the ledger this month?"

Debit and credit in one minute

Every posting has two sides that add up to the same amount. Debit = what we have or are owed goes up, or an expense goes up. Credit = income or what we owe goes up. When Mette is billed: debit "customers owe us", credit "premium income".

1. Sub-ledger and general ledger, in simple words

FSCD is a sub-ledger: it keeps one line per customer, policy and event. The GL keeps only totals per account. Each night FSCD adds up the day's postings and sends the totals to the GL. The GL account that holds "all customers' open items" is called the reconciliation account: its balance must always equal the total of the open items in FSCD.

The golden rule: total of FSCD open items = balance of the reconciliation account in the GL.

2. Account determination: how FSCD picks the GL account

Nobody types a GL account into FSCD. The account is found automatically by account determination: a set of configuration tables that turn "what kind of money is this?" into "which GL accounts?". The main inputs are the company code, the division or product line, and the main and sub transaction from Lesson 1.

Why this matters for a BA

Every new kind of money (a new fee, a new product, a new tax) needs a new main/sub transaction and its account determination. If it's missing, the posting fails or lands on a wrong account. Always ask finance: "which GL accounts, and is it taxable?" before the developers start.

Written premium vs earned premium

FSCD posts the premium when it's billed (written premium). Accountants also need earned premium: by 30.06 only 3 of Mette's 12 months have passed. That split (the unearned premium reserve) is usually calculated in finance or actuarial systems, not in FSCD. Good to know when finance asks "why is FSCD income higher than the P&L?"

5. The GL transfer

The GL transfer (FPG1) is a batch job, usually nightly. It takes all closed reconciliation keys that haven't been transferred yet, adds up their lines per GL account, and posts the totals as GL documents. Finance then sees one line like "premium income, 01.04, DKK 2.4 million" instead of 40,000 customer lines.

  • Summarised, but traceable: from the GL document you can go back to the reconciliation key, and from the key to every FSCD document.
  • Once only: a key is transferred once. Reversals and corrections come in new keys on later days.
  • If it fails: usually a missing GL account, a closed posting period in the GL, or a missing cost center or profit center. The key stays "not transferred" until it's fixed and the job runs again.

6. Reconciling: does FSCD equal the ledger?

Reconciliation proves the golden rule from section 1. Finance compares the open items list in FSCD on a key date (for example FPO1, open items at 31.05) with the balance of the reconciliation accounts in the GL on the same date.

7. Month-end and year-end checklist

  1. All batches done: payment runs, payment lots, returns lots, dunning and interfaces for the month have finished.
  2. Clarification cleared: unmatched payments are assigned or handled by an agreed rule.
  3. Keys closed and transferred: every reconciliation key for the month is closed and in the GL.
  4. Foreign currency valuation: open items in a foreign currency (for example SEK policies in a Danish company) are revalued at the month-end rate, so the DKK value in the books is current.
  5. Receivables ageing and bad-debt provision: finance looks at how old the open items are (0–30, 31–60, 61–90, 90+ days) and sets aside money for what probably won't be paid.
  6. Write-offs: small and hopeless debts are written off (Lesson 3), so the books don't carry money that will never come.
  7. Reconcile and sign off: FSCD open items = GL reconciliation accounts. Finance closes the period.
Year-end extras

At 31.12 finance usually also checks insurance tax payable against the tax return, credit balances owed to customers (old overpayments, unclaimed refunds), and that no postings are made into the closed year after sign-off.

8. BA toolkit for finance integration

8.1 Questions to ask for any new posting

  1. Which main and sub transaction will it use? Is it new?
  2. Which GL accounts (receivable or payable side, and revenue or expense side)? Who in finance signs this off?
  3. Is it taxable? Which tax account?
  4. Does it need a cost center, profit center or product line for reporting?
  5. Which reconciliation key will it be posted under, and from which process?
  6. How will finance see it in their reports, and how will it be reconciled?

A closing tip

Ask finance for last month's reconciliation file and walk through it once with them. Seeing which keys, accounts and differences they check teaches you more than any document.
